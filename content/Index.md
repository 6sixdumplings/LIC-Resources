# The Sermon on the Mount: Kingdom Character, Witness, and Heart Ethics


Welcome to the Sermon on the Mount Collection**. This index serves as both a narrative roadmap through Jesus' foundational teaching in **Matthew 5:3–32** and a central jump site to access full sermon manuscripts, bilingual study editions, presentation slides, and PDF transcripts.


---

## 📖 The Narrative Arc of Matthew 5

The Sermon on the Mount represents Jesus’ definitive manifesto on what it means to live as a citizen of the Kingdom of Heaven. Rather than offering a loose collection of moral maxims, Jesus lays out a tightly integrated, progressive framework for heart transformation.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                          THE NARRATIVE FLOW                             │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. KINGDOM CHARACTER (Matt 5:3-12)                                      │
│    Internal transformation through the Beatitudes                      │
│    └─► Poor in Spirit ──► Mourning ──► Meekness ──► Hungering           │
│                                                                         │
│ 2. KINGDOM WITNESS (Matt 5:13-16)                                       │
│    Public impact of transformed citizens                                │
│    ├─► Salt of the Earth (Moral Preservation & Flavor)                 │
│    └─► Light of the World (Visible Good Deeds for God's Glory)         │
│                                                                         │
│ 3. KINGDOM ETHICS (Matt 5:21-32)                                        │
│    Radical heart-level righteousness replacing legalistic loopholes     │
│    ├─► Anger & Bitterness ──► Urgent Reconciliation (5:21-26)          │
│    ├─► Lust & Covetousness ──► Radical Mortification of Sin (5:27-30)   │
│    └─► Marital Breakdown   ──► Covenant Faithfulness (5:31-32)         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Kingdom Character: The Beatitudes (Matt 5:3–12)
Jesus begins not with commands, but with blessings (*macarisms*). The Beatitudes describe the inner heart dispositions of kingdom citizens. They form connected pairs:
* **The Needy Heart**: Those who are *poor in spirit* (v.3) realize their spiritual bankruptcy, leading to *showing mercy* (v.7).
* **The Repentant Heart**: Those who *mourn over sin* (v.4) are purified within, becoming *pure in heart* (v.8).
* **The Humble Heart**: The *meek* (v.5) yield their self-will, becoming active *peacemakers* (v.9).
* **The Righteous Pursuit**: Those who *hunger and thirst for righteousness* (v.6) inevitably face *persecution for righteousness* (v.10).

### 2. Kingdom Witness: Salt and Light (Matt 5:13–16)
Character naturally overflows into public testimony. Jesus bestows upon His disciples two vivid identities:
* **Salt of the Earth (v.13)**: Restraining moral decay in a fallen world while enriching society with spiritual truth. Losing "saltiness" (*mōranthē*) means blending in until the testimony becomes worthless.
* **Light of the World (v.14–16)**: Functioning like a city set on a hill. Faith must not be hidden under a basket; rather, visible good deeds (*kalos*) shine to draw observers to glorify the Father in heaven.

### 3. Kingdom Ethics: Heart Transformation (Matt 5:21–32)
Addressing the prevailing legalism of His day, Jesus introduces the radical formula: *"You have heard that it was said... but I tell you."* True righteousness is not merely external rule-keeping, but a matter of the heart:
* **Anger & Reconciliation (v.21–26)**: Harbingers of murder begin in unresolved resentment and contemptuous insults (*Raca*). Worship is unacceptable while holding unaddressed grievances with a brother; reconciliation must be pursued immediately.
* **Lust & Adultery (v.27–30)**: Adultery begins in the eye and mind. Jesus commands radical, decisive action—metaphorically gouging out the eye or cutting off the hand—to sever sin at its source before it destroys the soul.
* **Marriage & Covenant Faithfulness (v.31–32)**: Rebutting legalistic loopholes that abused Moses' divorce allowances, Jesus upholds God's original creation design (*Leave, Cleave, One Flesh*), calling believers to lifelong fidelity and grace.
