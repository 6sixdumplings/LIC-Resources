## 1. What the teachers argued  (Matt 5:31, 19:3, Deut 24:1).

#### Read Matt 5:31
>31 “It has been said, ‘Anyone who divorces his wife must give her a certificate of divorce.’ 

Jesus here responds to the culture of the day where divorce had become very easy and common. 

Bible historian, Josephus in his book the Jewish Antiquities, notes that at this time "divorce was purely a male prerogative, which required no legal hearing, merely the husband's decision; Jewish law made no provision for a woman to initiate divorce"(Josephus, *Ant.* 15.259, cited in France 2007, 207).

So there were two Rabbinical schools of thought concerning divorce: 

Firstly,  the  House of Shammai had a strict or conservative view of marriage.  

They taught that divorce was permissible only for a major sexual offense or unchastity (Barbieri 1985, 31; Stott 1985, 93).

They argued from Deut 24:1-4 that a man could divorce his  wife for  "for any and every reason  (*kat' aitian pasan*" / Deut 24:1 *'erwat dābār*).

Even for a  minor domestic displeasures like burning a meal or losing her physical beauty.

We see this controversy illustrated when the Pharisees tried to set a trap for Jesus with this question about divorces in Matt 19:3

## 2. What Moses actually allowed (Deut (24:1-4)

#### a. Moses permitted divorce in cases of sexual infidelity

Moses acknowledged the reality and destructive nature of marital breakdown, but he only allowed it only for cases of sexual infidelity, not for any and every reason.  

A careful reading the Hebrew text here clearly shows that the displeasure of the husband, is connected only with an sexual indecency, and not a displeasure for any or every reason. 

The term “indecency” translates  the Hebrew word עֶרְוָה‎ (ěr·wāh), which literally means the shameful exposing of her nakedness. 

This word is an euphemism for illicit sex (Swanson 1997, 6872).

The this law then described a situation where  man is permitted to write his wife a certificate of divorce when clear evidence emerged that there had been sexual infidelity before marriage, or adultery during marriage. 

Joseph found himself in this predicament when he discovered Mary’s pregnancy, and consequently resolved to quietly divorce her (Matthew 1:18-25).  

Notice further that..

####  b. Moses didn’t command divorce, he mitigated its harmful effects.

Moses also does not command divorce in this case law,  he merely recognised the reality of it, and mitigated it's harmful effects (Deut 24:2-4).

If you carefully read this law you will notice it's flow and structure as:  "when...if...if...if...then, because"  flow of the passage (vs 1-4):   

(1) **When** ...a man marries a wife...
(2) **If** ...he discovers the indecency of sexual sin, 
(3) **and if**... he writes her a bill of divorce
(3) **and if** ...she remarries 
(4) and if... she divorce again / or her 2nd husband dies.
(5) **then** ...the original husband may not take her again as his wife 
(6) **because..**will be defiled 

Notice while recognising the validity of divorce in the case of sexual infidelity before or during the marriage, the only command here is actually against remarring the original spouse, after being married to a second husband. 

Moses then gave this law as a civil concession, not a command.

####  c.  The  certificate of divorce was given not as incentive, but as deterrent to divorce

Although the law prescribed an certificate of divorce in case of divorce, it was never intended as an incentive for divorce. 

Instead it was designed as a kind of cooldown period, and opportunity to carefully consider the grave consequences and finality of a divorce. 

Matthew Henry notes...
>"Requiring a written instrument executed before witnesses forced angry men to act deliberately rather than chasing their wives out in a temporary fit of passion (Henry 1994, 1633).

In 2021 the Chinese authorities implemented Article 1077 of the 2021 Civil Code, mandating a 30-day "cooling-off period" for consensual administrative divorces. This simple act reduced the divorce rate with nearly 2.6 million  divorces annually, down from 4.5 million a year.   (Zheng, Niu, and Chen 2024).

The written notice (*biblion / apostasion*) also protected the discarded wife against the angry whim of her husband who might otherwise cast her out with no legal proof of her single status (Robertson 1933). It certified that she was legally freed from his house and authority (Calvin and Pringle 2010, 292).

**Adultery** strikes at the heart of God's design for marriage.

**Divorce** likewise falls short of God's creational intent.

The very language of Scripture recognizes the painful reality of two lives being separated after having become "one flesh."

One of the lesser-discussed tragedies of premarital sexual relationships and repeated cohabiting relationships is that destroys a person's ability to "cleave" wholeheartedly to a future spouse. 

Every intimate relationship leaves an imprint. Every breakup carries the potential for emotional wounds, guardedness, mistrust, and relational patterns that are brought into future marriages.

## 4. What Jesus said (Matt 5:32, 19:9, Luke )

With this background in mind let us return to Jesus's words in the Sermon on the Mount in Matt 5:31-32 on divorce and remarriage. 

#### a. Jesus Upholds Sexual Immorality (_Porneia_) as a Legitimate Ground for Divorce (5:32; 19:9; cf. Luke 16:18)

Jesus upholds the teaching of Moses by recognizing sexual immorality as a legitimate ground for divorce. While divorce is permitted in such circumstances, it is never commanded.

> "But I tell you that anyone who divorces his wife, except for marital unfaithfulness, causes her to become an adulteress..." (Matt. 5:32)

The word translated "marital unfaithfulness" is _porneia_, a broad term referring to sexual immorality. 

In the context of marriage, it includes adultery and other serious forms of sexual sin that violate the marriage covenant.

At the same time, we must be careful not to encourage divorce too quickly where adultery or sexual unfaithfulness has occurred. 

A spouse who has suffered such betrayal will often need significant support, wisdom, and discernment from fellow believers and church leaders.

On the one hand, adultery is not an unforgivable sin. Christ died for adulterers as surely as He died for every other sinner. Through genuine repentance, He is able to forgive, restore, and heal what has been deeply broken.

On the other hand, sexual unfaithfulness strikes at the very heart of marriage. By becoming one flesh with someone other than one's spouse, the adulterer commits a profound violation of the covenant that God established.

Therefore, wherever possible, every effort should be made to pursue repentance, forgiveness, and reconciliation. 

Yet Jesus recognizes that persistent and unrepentant sexual immorality may so fundamentally rupture the marriage covenant that divorce becomes a legitimate option before God.

### b. Jesus Rejects Divorce for Any and Every Reason (5:32; 19:9; cf. Luke 16:18)

**Read Matthew 5:31-32**

> **31** “It has been said, ‘Anyone who divorces his wife must give her a certificate of divorce.’  32 But I tell you that anyone who divorces his wife, except for marital unfaithfulness, causes her to become an adulteress, and anyone who marries the divorced woman commits adultery.”

Remember that Jesus is responding to the attitude and approach of the permissive culture of His day. He firmly rejects the practice of divorcing for any and every reason.

Jesus' points in verse 32 is that when a husband divorces his wife without legitimate grounds, he not only breaks covenant faithfulness himself, but he places her in a situation that can lead to adultery.

This may happen in several ways:

**(1)** While the husband may have served her with a certificate of divorce, in the absence of legitimate grounds for divorce, the marriage union may still be valid in God's sight. A human document cannot necessarily dissolve what God has joined together.

**(2)** By divorcing her and sending her away, he places her in circumstances where the marriage covenant is treated as though it has been dissolved, when in God's sight it may still remain intact.

**(3)** As a result, whether through financial necessity, social vulnerability, or the longing for companionship, she may eventually enter into a relationship or marriage with another man. Yet if the original marriage still stands in God's sight, this would constitute adultery.

**(4)** Likewise, if the original divorce lacked biblical grounds, the man who marries her also commits adultery, because in God's eyes the original marriage covenant still stands.

Although Jesus presents this example from the perspective of a husband divorcing his wife, we know that in many societies today it is often the wife who initiates the divorce. The principle, however, remains the same.

**Read Mark 10:11-12**

> **11** He answered, “Anyone who divorces his wife and marries another woman commits adultery against her.  
> **12** And if she divorces her husband and marries another man, she commits adultery.”

Jesus therefore applies the same standard to both husbands and wives. The issue is not whether the man or the woman initiates the divorce, but whether the marriage covenant is being broken contrary to God's will. God calls both husbands and wives to covenant faithfulness and rejects the practice of treating marriage as something that can be dissolved whenever it becomes difficult or inconvenient.

## Conclusion and Summary

Dear friends as we conclude with this sobering teaching of Christ,  I would like to make a few practical applications from this passage. 

#### (1) Let us stop seeking loopholes in Scripture to justify our sin.  

Instead, let us bring our hardness of heart before God,  asking the Holy Spirit to enable us to respond with forgiveness and grace to our spouse. 

We should ask  _"What sin in our marriage needs to be repented of so we can stay together?"_ rather than _"How can I get out of it? 

####  **2. Let us actively safeguard the creation pattern in our marriage:   Leave, Cleave, and Become One Flesh**

We can protect our marriages when we intentionally activate all three dimensions of leaving, cleaving and becoming one flesh: 
    1. **Properly Leaving:** We might need to establish appropriate bounderies from parents, or external dependencies so that our marriage and household may not be undermined.
    2. **Faithfully Cleaving (** **Kollao** **):** Let us repent from, and  guard our hearts against premarital or extramarital emotional attachments that impair deep relational bonding
    3. **Nourishing One-Flesh Unity:** Protecting both body and mind from objectifying lust (Matt 5:28) and treating one's spouse as an image-bearer of God to be cherished[1].

### **3. Let us respond with truth and grace to those who experience the trauma of divorce**

As a church we must not adopt a cold Pharisaical stance toward those who are going through or had experienced a divorce.  The heartace and trauma of divorce calls for a compassionate, caring and supportive respons to walk with fellow believers whose marriages may be in trouble or are breaking up.  

#### 4. Pray together as a family and a couple. 

The saying, a family that prays together stays together seem to hold truth.  Academic research show that couples who practice their faith together - attending church together, serving together, and praying together experience more satisfying marriage relationships.
