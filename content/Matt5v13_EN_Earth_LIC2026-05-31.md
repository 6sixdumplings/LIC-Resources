#  1. Review: Blessed are the Peacemakers and those who are Persecuted for Righteousness  (5:9-12)

Firstly, we explored the blessings of peacemaking and persecution (5:9-12).  

Secondly, this morning we move on to  **Matt 5:13-16** where Jesus compares the identity and calling of his disciples with being the "Salt of the earth (v14) and the "Light of the Word (v14-16). [^2] 

It is important to pause a moment to reflect on how this new section (v13-16) is connected with the beatitudes (v3-9) and especially these last two beatitudes (v9-12).   

It will become clear that the very attitudes and characteristics of the citizens of Christ's kingdom, as outlined in the beatitudes, will give disciples of Christ their distinctiveness and impact in the world. 

We have also seen in the immediate context of vs 10-12 that the world is hostile against Christ, his disciples, our distinctive lifestyle and our Gospel message. 

As a result, Christ's disciples might be tempted to hide their identity and retreat or disengage from the world in fear of opposition. 

On the other hand, we may be tempted to blend into the world so much that we become worldly and, as a result, lose our distinctiveness of life and witness.  

 By underlining our identity and calling as the "Salt of the Earth" (v14) and the "Light of the Word (v14-16), Jesus now urges us as his disciples to maintain our distinctiveness,  and fulfil our purpose in the world by being "different and be seen to be different” [^3].

#  2. Disciples of Christ are the Salt of the Earth (5:13)。


>“You are the salt of the earth. But if the salt loses its saltiness, how can it be made salty again? It is no longer good for anything, except to be thrown out and trampled by men" (Matt 5:13 NIV)

## 2.1 Our calling to be "the salt of the earth"(v13a)

Notice that Jesus bestows upon all his disciples this unique calling to be   "the salt of the earth" (v13a)!≈

>13a “You are the salt f the earth" 

This is a gracious gift that all believers share as our collective identity and calling "salt of the earth" [^6].  

The verb form ‘you are’ (Ὑμεῖς ἐστε) indicates that it is a present and active ministry that all followers of Christ participate in. 

Being the salt of the earth is a gracious, present, collective, and active ministry to the world until Christ returns. 

### Salt preserves and add flavor

Salt was essential for human well-being in Jesus’ day. Its primary use was preserving and enhancing food’s taste. 

Since people lacked freezers, they salted their food to prevent spoilage and add flavor. 

Thus, salt served as a crucial means of both preservation and  adding taste. 

The question then is how Jesus imagines his disciples to be instruments of preservation and flavor in the world? (v13).

In the OT there are also examples of salt being used for sacrificial and medicinal purposes (Lev 2:13)[^21]
### Jesus' disciples must restrain evil in the world 

Most of the bible commentators reflect on the restraining influence that Christians ought to have on evil and moral decline in the world.  

Without the godly influence of the church of Christ, and individual disciples of Christ the world be in utter darkness.  

Without believers in the world the speed and extend of moral decay and corruption would greatly accelerate. 

The bible commentator Matthew Poole writes that if it were not for the salty influence of believers the 'world would be a stinking dunghill..that would be a stench in the nostrils of the pure and holy God'[^7]

So despite it's hostility to us as Christ's disciples,  the world left to it self is decaying "like rotten fish or meat". [^9]  While God has given also graciously instituted the government to restrain evil in the world, but God’s people are the most powerful bring flavour to the world and to restrain evil.and God's own "redeemed, regenerate and righteous people",  are the most "the '"most powerful restraint"[^9] 

### Disciples must add taste to the world 

Given the context here, Jesus may also and particularly had in mind the flavoring property of salt.[^5] 

Believers should add flavor to the world, in the way we live and speak[^10]

The apostle Paul draws applies Jesus' image of salt when he writes *"Let your conversation be always full of grace, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how to answer everyone"* (Col 4:6 NIV)*

When believers of Christ live out the distinctive lifestyles as demonstrated in the Beatitudes (Matt 5:3-10) and expounded upon in the Sermon on the Mount (Matt 5-7), it will no doubt generate the hostility of ungodly culture.  Yet among them there will be those who are attracted and who will begin to ask us to give a reason for the hope we have in Christ. 

That is why Peter writes in the same vein *"Always be prepared to give an answer to everyone who asks you to give the reason for the hope that you have. But do this with gentleness and respect" (1 Peter 3:15 NIV)* 

So as we live out our distinctive Christian life styles we make (1) restrain evil in the world, (2) make the world a better place[^11], (3) indeed we hold out the gospel of salvation for God's elect. 

We as disciples of Christ are therefore essential to the wellbeing and salvation of the world! [^12]

### Reflect, Discus & Apply

How can we, as individual Christians, and we as a church collectively,  restrain moral decline in our communities, while at the same time adding the the flavor of God grace and salvation? 

## 2.2 The risk of losing our saltiness  (13b)

*“You are the salt of the earth. But if the salt loses its saltiness, how can it be made salty again? It is no longer good for anything, except to be thrown out and trampled by men" (Matt 5:13 NIV)*

Despite the high calling and privilege of being the 'salt of the earth' (v13), there remains a risk to disciples of Christ that we might loose our saltiness or impact in the world (13b)

>13b but if salt has lost its taste,

The GK μωρανθῇ (Vb.Aor.Pas.Sub.3PS) verb form here indicates the possibility that believers instead of influencing the world,  be so influenced by the world that we become insipid or tasteless.  

Pure salt or "sodium chloride" is actually a "very stable chemical compound" which cannot loose its taste.[^16] 

However in Jesus' time salt consisted of an impure mixture of minerals from which the pure salt could potentially could wash out or dissolve. [^4] . 

As a result this 'salt mix' could then become insipid, tasteless,  [^14][^17] and useless  [^18]

**The foolishness of loosing our saltiness**

The GK word μωρανθῇ can also be translated as 'becoming foolish' which connects the loss of our saltiness as an act of foolishness on the part of the believer.[^13]

King David, while generally were a man of God,  lost his senses and acted foolishly when against the Word of the LORD took a census of his fighting men.  

This act revealed his trust in human strength rather than God's protection (2 Sam 24:10), which resulted in judgement upon David. [^19]

**Counting the cost of discipleship**

In the Gospel of Luke, Jesus connects the loosing of our saltiness with a failure to count the cost of discipleship (Luke 14:33-35).  

*"In the same way, any of you who does not give up everything he has cannot be my disciple.“Salt is good, but if it loses its saltiness, how can it be made salty again?  (**Luke 14:33-35 NIV**).

Having salt in ourselves, in this context,  means that our allegiance to Christ takes priority over our parents, children and indeed to our own lives . 

Just like a King who first estimates the cost of entry into a war,  or a builder who estimates the cost of a building project, so believers must count the cost of being followers of Christ. 

Failure to do so may leave us vulnerable to loosing our saltiness as soon as we face the pressure of persecution or the temptations of our evil desires, and of the world system.

Likewise in the Gospel of Mark Jesus warns us that those disciples who loose their saltiness will be "*salted with fire"* (Mark 9:39) 

The context of the Mark' passage is  the failure of disciples to deal decisively with sin.  Failure to cut of or plug out things that might cause ourselves or others to stumble. 

Causing *"one of these little ones"* (Mark 9:49 NIV) to fall into sin, may refer to an obstacle to the gospel to seekers and young believers, rather than making the gospel of Christ attractive through our lives and testimony. 

Jesus therefore urges believers to *"have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with each other”* (Mark 9:50 NIV).*

It is also noteworthy here that Jesus' urging to be *"at peace with each other"* resonates with the blessedness of the "*peacemakers"* (Matt 5:9).

When we as disciples of Christ are angry and in conflict with one another, we have are at risk of losing our saltiness and harming our testimony to the world.  

**Jesus seek a pure Church that will make an impact**

We may also discern that Jesus is concerned for the purity of church and the authenticity of his disciples. 

Remember the crowd that Jesus address in the Sermon on the Mount (Matt 5-7) were was a mixed audience.  People came to listen to him for a variety of reasons.  

Some because of curiosity,  other because of their personal need,  yet others to catch him out.  Only a relatively few would show them selves to be true disciples. 

**Application**
The Word of God then calls us this morning to make sure that we have 'salt in ourselves',  (1) that have responded to Christ in sincerity of heart, with a true faith,  and practical obedience;  (2) that we so follow Christ,  and live so distinctiely that we do not loose our essential character whereby we as believers should impact the world.  [^20] 

John Stott writes 

>“Christian saltiness is Christian character as depicted in the beatitudes, committed Christian discipleship exemplified in both deed and word” (Stott 1985, 60 § Matt 5:15

>“If Christians become assimilated to non-Christians and contaminated by the impurities of the world, they lose their influence” (Stott 1985, 60 § Matt 5:15).


Dr Martin Lloyd-Jones points out that only when we are distinct from the world we become attractive to the world

>"The glory of the gospel is that when the Church is absolutely different from the world, she invariably attracts it. It is then that the world is made to listen to her message, though it may hate it at first” (Stott 1985, 60 § Matt 5:15)
	

**Reflect, Discuss and Apply** 

> [!NOTE] Discuss
In what ways may you as an individual disciple be at risk of losing your saltiness, and how can we as a church avoid forfeiting our impact in our community? 


## 2.3 The warning not to become worthless in Christ's Kingdom (13c)

We have now seen in 5:13 (1) our calling as disciples of Christ to be the salt of the earth; and  (2) the risk of losing our saltiness. Notice (3) also Jesus' warning against becoming useless or worthless to his Kingdom.  

*“You are the salt of the earth. But if the salt loses its saltiness, how can it be made salty again? **It is no longer good for anything, except to be thrown out and trampled by men"** (Matt 5:13 NIV)*

Jesus' rhetorical question *"how shall its saltiness be restored*  (13c)" is designed to remind us that once our saltiness is gone, it's either difficult or imposible to regain. 

>13c how shall its saltiness be restored?[^22] [^23]

It might be the some followers have lost it's saltiness simply because they have never been true disciples of Christ.  

We know that some followers, although they seem to start out well in their following of Christ,  the seed fell on on the rocks or among the thorns.  

As a result when persecutions, the pressure of life, and the deceitfulness of wealth comes along (Matt 13:22) it chokes their faith, and they turn away from Christ disappointed.  

It is at this point where they have lost their saltiness in God's kingdom, and the point Jesus makes is that having first followed him, only to turn their back upon him, makes the worthless in his Kingdom.   

It might also be that Jesus  likely believers who sincerely follows Christ,  yet because they have not dealt with sin in their lives they have lost their distinctiveness in God's kingdom.  As a result their Christian integrity and witness may be destroyed.  Once destroyed it either difficult or impossible to regain.  

Our witness will be  "*no longer good for anything except to be thrown out and trampled under people’s feet.* " (13d )

Unbelievers despise the hypocrisy of people who claim to be Christian, and yet their lives shout they they are not. 

This also reminds us of the the parable of the vine (John 15) where the Father cuts of the branches that bears no fruit.  So what we have here then is a very sober warning, to live as true disciples of Christ,  and be faithful to our calling to have salt in ourselves.
