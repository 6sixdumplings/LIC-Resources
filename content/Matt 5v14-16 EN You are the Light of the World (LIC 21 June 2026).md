#GOM/Matt5 
# You are the Light of the World (Matthew 5:14-16)


>[!Bible] [Matthew 5:14-16 NIV](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/MAT.5.14-16)
>“You are the light of the world\. A town built on a hill cannot be hidden\. Neither do people light a lamp and put it under a bowl\. Instead they put it on its stand, and it gives light to everyone in the house\. In the same way, let your light shine before others, that they may see your good deeds and glorify your Father in heaven\.


*The Beatitudes (Matt 5:3-12)* describe the attitudes and character of the citizens of the Kingdom of Heaven. The values and lifestyles of Christ’s disciples, as citizens of the *Kingdom of Heaven*, are the complete opposite of those of the Kingdom of the Earth. As a result, those who belong to the *Kingdom of this World* tend to react with hostility towards Christ, his message, and his disciples (Matt 5:9-12). 

>“Blessed are you when people insult you, persecute you and falsely say all kinds of evil against you because of me" Matt (5:12 NIV)

Hostility is often expressed through disbelief in our message, unbelief in God, and negative attitudes towards Christ, the Gospel, the church, or Christians.  Sometimes, this hostility escalates to slander, discrimination, or even persecution of believers.  I am sure you have seen or experienced all of this.   For this very reason, Jesus reminded us that despite the hostility of *The World* and the potential for persecution, we, as disciples of Christ, must live out our identity and calling as the salt of the earth.

Previously, I pointed out that we are called to be the salt of the earth, as stated in v13a.

>“You are the salt of the earth. But if the salt loses its saltiness, how can it be made salty again? It is no longer good for anything, except to be thrown out and trampled underfoot" (Matt 5:13 NIV).

Firstly,  I pointed out that we as disciples of Christ are *CALLED*  "to be the salt of the earth" (v13) in that we restrain evil in a world corrupted by sin and in a downward spiral of decay.  We are also called to be the ‘salt of the earth,’ by adding the flavour of Christ and his Gospel to the world.  Our Christian lifestyle makes the Gospel appealing to a dying world, and it prompts unbelievers to ask for the reason of our hope (1 Pet 3:15). 

Secondly, we have seen that there is a risk that we, as disciples of Christ, may be losing our saltiness. Saltiness refers to the character, values and lifestyles that Jesus put forward in the beatitudes. 

Thirdly, Jesus warned us (v13) not to conform to the world’s values, attitudes, and lifestyle and lose our distinctive lifestyle and impact.  This would render us worthless in God's Kingdom! Just like Lot's wife, who so loved and longed for her life in Sodom that she ended up being judged along with Sodom. 

To be the salt of the earth is noble and a serious calling.  We must so guard our Christian character and witness that we will not become worthless, having no impact for Christ in the World

Let us now turn to Jesus' teaching that "Disciples of Christ are the Light of the Word" in Matthew 5:14-16.  

>"14 “You are the light of the world. A city on a hill cannot be hidden. 15 Neither do people light a lamp and put it under a bowl. Instead, they put it on its stand, and it lights up everyone in the house.16 In the same way, let your light shine before men, that they may see your good deeds and praise your Father in heaven" (Matt 5:14-16 NIV)

I would like to highlight the following points: 

1. You are the Light of the World (v14).
2. We cannot hide our light (v15).
3. So, we must shine our light (v16).


# 1. You are the Light of the World (14a)

>“You are the light of the world" (Matt 5:14a )

Light (φῶς) here stands in contrast to darkness, and often refers to a particular source of light such as the sun, moon, fire or a lamp[^3]. Jesus thus states that all his disciples, all believers - irrespective of race, age, gender, income, job, or social status - share in this precious identity and calling to be "*The Light of the World*" (v14a). This assumes that the world is still locked into darkness and in desperate need of the light that only Christ's disciples can bring! It also means that we draw on God as the source of light, and continue with the help of the Holy Spirit, Christ's ministry to be the Light of the World.  

The Old Testament background here gives us deeper insight into our calling to be the light of the world:

## 2.1 God is Light

When we look at the OT idea of "light',  we find that God is an everlasting light for his people **(Isaiah 60:19-20)**, and that we hope to dwell forever in God's light.  

>"19The sun will no more be your light by day, nor will the brightness of the moon shine on you, **for the Lord will be your everlasting** light, and your God will be your glory. 20 Your sun will never set again, and your moon will wane no more; **the Lord will be your everlasting light**, and your days of sorrow will end" (Isa 60:19 NIV)

The prophet Isaiah also prophesied that the *Servant of the Lord*, as the *Promised Messiah*, would be a" *Light to the Gentiles*" (Isa 42:6, 49:6).

>“I, the Lord,  have called you in righteousness; I will take hold of your hand. I will keep you and will make you to be a covenant for the people and a light for the Gentiles" (Isa 42:6 NIV)

It's important to see that being the *Light to the Gentiles*"  is explained as 'bringing my (the LORD's) salvation *To the Ends of the Earth.*’ The emphasis then is that God’s salvation is not limited to the Israelites; instead, His purpose is to bring His salvation to the ends of the earth  (Isa 42:6, 49:6). So the LORD God, Yahweh, and His *Promised Messiah*, the *Servant of the Lord, is the eternal light of salvation for all nations, to the ends of the earth.  


## 2.2 Christ is Light 

When we come to Matthew's Gospel, we see clearly that Jesus is the Servant of the Lord, the *Promised Messiah*,  who is the Light of the World. Shortly after *John the Baptist's* death, in Matt 4,   Jesus relocated to Galilee to begin his public ministry in the vicinity of the Lake of Galilee.  Matthew utilises "*Galilee of the Gentiles*" as an image or paradigm of the *"people living in darkness"*, in "*the shadow of death" (*Matt 5:15-16). 

>"15 “Land of Zebulun and land of Naphtali, the Way of the Sea, beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the Gentiles— 16 the people living in darkness have seen a great light; on those living in the land of the shadow of death a light has dawned" (Matt 5:15-16 NIV).

When Jesus talks about the *kosmos κόσμος*, he has in mind not the physical world in which we live.  Instead, he refers to the people of the world who are estranged from God, who belong to the world system. [^4]. "*People living in darkness, in the shadow of death* (Matt 5:15-16) is a very apt description of the world in which we live today. The question, of course, is who is the "*great light*" that the *people living in darkness* have seen (Matt 5:16)?  The Galileans, who lived in darkness, have witnessed a great light because Jesus relocated to their region and began his ministry among them (Matthew 5:15-16). The apostle John affirms Jesus' identity as the *Light of the Wo**rld* (John 1:5, 8:12 and 9:5).** 

>“In him was life, and that life was the light of men. The light shines in the darkness, but the darkness has not understood it" (John 1:4-5 NIV).

>“When Jesus spoke again to the people, he said, **“I am the light of the world.** Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness, but will have the light of life” (John 8:12 NIV)

>“While I am in the world, I am the light of the world” (John 9:15 NIV)

Jesus then is the the *great light* (Matt 5:6), the one '*who has the light of life in himself"* (John 1:4). He is the "*light that shines in the darkness'(John 1:5),  *the light of the world**" (John 8;12). Those who follow Jesus will "never walk in darkness"* (John 8:12)"


## 2.3 We are Light  

Not only is God our Light, but Jesus Christ, his Son, is also the Light for the World. We, his disciples, are also called to be and continue his ministry of being light in the world
### Disciples of Christ follow Jesus’ ministry of being the world’s light.

It is interesting to see that Jesus claims to be the *"Light of the Word"* (John 9:5) as long as he is in the world.  In other words, as soon as Jesus left the world, he transferred this ministry of being the *light of the world* to us, his disciples, empowered by the Holy Spirit. [^5]  

John Stott (1985, 61 § Matt 5:14) and France 2007, 176 § Matt 5:14)  both emphasise that Christ now shines his light through his disciples. 

> [!NOTE] Jesus Transferred His Ministry to be the Light of the World to His Disciples
> 
> "Old Testament prophecy that God’s Servant would be ‘a light to the nations’ is said to have been fulfilled not only in Christ himself, the light of the world, but also by Christians who bear witness to Christ" (Stott 1985, 61 § Matt 5:14)
> 
> “Here the light which Jesus brings is provided also by his disciples, who will soon be commissioned to share in his ministry of proclamation and deliverance” (France 2007, 176 § Matt 5:14). 
> 
> “The world needs that light, and it is through the disciples that it must be visible” (France 2007, 176 § Matt 5:14)

### Christ’s disciples, the New Israel, are called to be the light to the nations.

As disciples of Christ, we are appointed as the New Israel, called to be the Light of the Nations, to the ends of the earth. The prophet Isaiah considered not only the Servant of the Lord as a Light to the World, but Israel, the people of God, was particularly tasked to be a light to the Gentile nations (Isa 49:6)[^6] 

>“It is too small a thing for you to be my servant to restore the tribes of Jacob and bring back those of Israel I have kept. I will also make you a light for the Gentiles, that you may bring my salvation to the ends of the earth (Isa 49:6 NIV)”

So when Jesus designates us as the *Light of the World*, he also appoints us as his disciples to become the  *New Israel* who must take the *Good News of His Kingdom* to all nations, to the end of the earth. In other words, Jesus redefines who the true people of God are: Israel, as his disciples, we who believe and follow him. We who are living out His Kingdom values and character by faith in the Word. We are his people, and we are the Light to the World

### Stay connected with God to radiate your light.

It is evident, therefore, that the Father and the Son are the source of the light within us, and empower us to be light to the world through His Word and Spirit. We can do nothing in our own strength or power! This invites us into a deeper union with the Father and the Son through the Spirit. Only then will we shine brightly in a dark world. Even in the Old Testament, we see that the righteous are meant to be a radiant light in the world (Proverbs 4:18-19). 

>"The path of the righteous is like the first gleam of dawn, shining ever brighter till the full light of day. But the way of the wicked is like deep darkness they do not know what makes them stumble” (Prov 4:18-19 NIV).

As we grow deeper in the Word, prayer, and communion with the Father, Son, and Spirit, we will “shine” ever brighter until the full light of day (Prov 4:19).
### The Light of Hope and Salvation in Christ

Yet, you might be asking what exactly is the content of this light that believers must shine to the world?  The light that we shine is really the hope of God's new kingdom, and our salvation from sin and death through Christ. France (2007, 176 § Matt 5:14) writes “light symbolised the new hope which arose through Jesus’ preaching of the coming of God’s reign” (France 2007, 176 § Matt 5:14). So, then we saw that *You are the Light of the World* (14a).  


# 2. We Cannot Hide our Light   (14b-15a)


We have established our precious identity, calling, responsibility, and privilege as the Light of the World. Now Jesus emphasises that we should not hide it; in fact, it's absurd or even impossible to hide it.  Jesus illustrates this point with the metaphor of "a city on the hill"(14b), and "a lamp on a stand" (14b). Notice firstly...(14b)


## 2.1 We are a City on the Hill (14b)

>"14b A city on a hill cannot be hidden" (Matt 5:14).

Jesus presents a logical argument: a city on a hill is visible to everyone, and at night, it’s visible from afar. In fact, the GK here does not merely refer to a city on a hill, it actually says a city on a mountain (ὄρος) [^7]. If ever you decide to visit South Africa, you should come and see Table Mountain, which is situated right in the middle of Cape Town.  At night, the city shines a spotlight against the mountain, which beautifully illuminates the mountain for everyone to see. Just imagine for a moment the old city of Cape Town was actually built on top of Table Mountain (see picture)

It would be strikingly beautiful,  unmistakably visible, and impossible to hide.  Such a city on a hill cannot be hidden; it exists to be seen. [^8] Jesus no doubt had Jerusalem in mind, which was perched at 2500 feet.  Jerusalem as a city on a hill would have been a vivid picture in the crowd’s minds, who had likely seen its light at night from afar. This image of a city on a hill symbolises our collective impact as disciples and the church of Christ into the darkness of the world[^10]. 

> [!NOTE] The Church as the City on a Hill
> "The image is of a brightly lit city on a hill-top, representing the corporate effect of the combined ‘lights’ of individual disciples” (France 1994, 910 § Matt 5:14)

The commentator,  Keener (1993, § Matt 5:14), points out that in Jewish OT thinking,  Jerusalem, just like Israel as a nation, was considered to be a Light to the World (Is 42:6; 49:6).[^9] And so what Jesus might be implying here is that his disciples collectively, as the church of Christ, are the New Jerusalem,  the true city of God. 

People should see our light from afar and come seeking the truth of the Good News of the Kingdom. It also calls us as a church to avoid the temptation to withdraw or disengage from the world in fear of opposition or persecution.  At the same time, it warns us against so blending and integrating with the world that our light likewise becomes darkness,  and we lose our ability to shine.

## 2.2 You are a Lamp on a Stand (15)

Jesus now compares his disciples to a third image, namely a *Lamp on a Stand* (see v15a)

>"Neither do people light a lamp and put it under a bowl" (Matt 5:15a)

Jesus, in his teaching, often draws on household items that are familiar to everyone.[^12]  In vs. 15, he refers to a lamp that can be a candle on a candlestick (λύχνος luchnos), or a small wicker oil lamp [^1]. He also mentions a bushel or bowl, which was used as a grain measure and was found in everyone’s house.[^13] Jesus' thinking is moving away from the impressive "City on a Hill" image, that represent our collective witness in the world. 

His thoughts now turn to just the single flame of the candle or the oil lamp. Although not particularly strong or impressive, a single flame still has the ability to light up a dark room. This reminds us that even though we as individual believers in Christ might not have a very strong or powerful light to shine, we can still be the light of Christ in our sphere of influence - there we live, work, study, or play.  We might feel we do not have much to offer, our light is so small and insignificant - yet in fact, no matter how small the light, light still drives out darkness.  

 During the COVID-19 pandemic (2020-2022), our country faced severe electricity shortages and daily power outages, up to 3-4 four times a day.  Many evenings, my family had supper or family devotions by candlelight or oil lamps. Since we only had two lamps, I often checked them during the day to ensure they had enough oil and that the wicks were still long enough. I strategically placed the lamps, ready to illuminate the room as soon as the electricity went out. This arrangement allowed us to continue with our supper, family devotions, or homework even when the lights suddenly went out. It would have been absurd and utterly ineffective to “*light a lamp and put it under a bowl”* (Matthew 5:15). That would have left us in darkness, preventing us from continuing with our work or family fellowship.  
 
 This is Jesus' point: it is absurd for us as his disciples to, for whatever reason, hide our light from the world!  It completely defeats the purpose of our salvation. Instead, Jesus says,  we must highlight our light by putting it on a stand (v15b). 

>Instead, they put it on its stand (Matt 5:15b NIV)

The stand (λυχνία) was designed to hold a single oil lamp or perhaps a few candles [^11]. Homes in Jesus’ day were naturally dark with few windows, so the small oil lamp had to be strategically placed on a lamp stand to maximise light (Keener 1993, § Matt 5:15-16).[^14]  It is therefore unthinkable that we as Christ's disciples would willingly hide our light, either in fear of opposition or compromise with the world. The Word of God teaches here that even though our light might be relatively weak and have a limited reach, we must position our light in our sphere of influence to maximise its reach and impact.  Notice the purpose in vs 15c:

>..and it gives light to everyone in the house.(Matt 5:15c NIV)

This idea of light flooding every corner of the room or the house, connects well with the example of  Mary who poured out the expensive pure nard oil on Jesus' feet, and then  filled the house with the fragrance of the perfume (John 12:3)

It's an example of how an act of devotion of Christ can tangibly shine light into darkness and bring about the fragrance of Christ, if you allow me to mix these metaphors.

> Given your current circumstances, including limitations in time and capacity, how can you best make an impact in the sphere of influence that Christ has placed 

Firstly, we are the Light of the World (v14a), God is Light, Christ is  Light, we are light. Secondly, we cannot hide our light  (14b-15a) because we are a city on a hill (v14b）and we are a lamp on a stand  (v15)


# 3. So, Shine Your Light! (v16)

Thirdly, so shine your light, with good deeds, to the glory of the Father (16b).

> "In the same way, let your light shine before men.." (Matt 5:16a NIV)

The words **"in the same way" (οὕτως )** build on the example of placing a lamp strategically on a lampstand to be seen[^15].  The GK verb λαμψάτω is an imperative, or a twofold command, namely (1) to produce light, and (2) to display our light.[^16] So our Lord Jesus Christ, the King of the universe, your Lord and Master, commands you to let the light of life within you shine through His Spirit. 

He also commands you not to hide that light, but to continuously display it clearly so that your life and witness shine brightly in every sphere of your life[^17]. In the context of Matt 5:13-16, the very act of hiding your light, or putting it under a bowl, might suffocate it for a lack of oxygen. 

>"As shining light to a dying world, we, as the disciples of Jesus, are not to conceal the truth we know or the truth of what we are. We are not to pretend to be other than we are, but be willing for our Christianity to be visible to all” (Stott 1985, 61 § Matt 5:14-16).

Putting our light on display in the world will make it burn more brightly. However, when hiding will suffocate it and make your faith unproductive[^24]. Let us take care that our light does not grow dim because of our (1) a lack of concern for the loss; (2) the comfort of worldliness and the temptation to want to simply blend in; (3) the fear of opposition or persecution.


## 3.1 Before Men (Matt 5:16, 6:1)

Notice also that the command is to shine your light **"before men"**(ἔμπροσθεν τῶν ἀνθρώπων)" to be seen by them.  The purpose of shining our light before unbelievers is so that they might find the Gospel attractive because they see our lives and hear our witness. Yet it might be a little confusing to read on to Matt 6:1, where Jesus warns against doing our acts of righteousness before men to be seen. [^18]

>“Be careful not to do your ‘acts of righteousness’ before men, to be seen by them. If you do, you will have no reward from your Father in heaven" (Matt 6:1 NIV).

So, should we do our works of righteousness before men or not?  The conflict in principle here is only on the surface.  We need to distinguish between true works of righteousness done for Christ, as opposed to acts of self-righteousness done only for the praise of men. There is a whole world of difference between the true works of righteousness done by Christ's disciples for the glory of God and acts of false righteousness done for the praise of men. [^19][^34][^35] In Matt 6:1, Jesus warns the Pharisees who were doing empty and legalistic works of righteousness. Their righteous acts were not accompanied by a genuine inner godliness, nor by a sincere desire that God would be glorified and the salvation of men. 


## 3.2 With Good Deeds (16a)

With that caution in mind, Jesus is clear that we should shine our light through our good deeds before men, so that they might see our distinctive lives. [^20]

> ..that they may see your good deeds..(Matt 6:1 NIV).

Certainly, the good (καλός) works that Jesus has in mind include works that flow from a good moral Christian character.[^21] Jesus doesn’t explicitly state what these good deeds are. However, in the context of Matthew 5-7, he might have been referring to the values, attitudes, and behaviours flowing out of the beatitudes. 

> [!What Good Deeds?]
 “The phrase “good deeds” conveys the qualities set out in the beatitudes, and especially the “righteousness” of life which is to be characteristic of disciples (Matt 5:6, 10, 20) (France 2007, 177 § Matt 5:16)

In addition, the good works flow from an obedient heart, as he makes clear in the *Sermon on the Mount (Matt 5-7).* When we truly live out the law of God as taught by Christ in will be evident in their relationships, sexual purity, healthy marriages, obedient children, and tangible good deeds of love and compassion in the community. Yet I agree with Stott (1985, 61 § Matt 5:14) who argues that 

>"It seems that ‘good works’ is a general expression to cover everything a Christian says and does because he is a Christian, every outward and visible manifestation of his Christian faith" (Stott 1985, 61 § Matt 5:14) 

Given that throughout Scripture light is also a symbol of truth, good works include our 'spoken testimony', and doing the work of evangelism (Stott 1985, 61 § Matt 5:14-16). [^22] While unbelievers might still be hostile to the light of our Christian lives and witness, when they see our loving community within the church, our loving marriages, and families marked by peace and joy - they will be amazed and praise God. The apostle Paul encourages us to speak and act in a way that makes us shine like stars in the darkness of a crooked and depraved world (Philippians 2:14-16).

>“Do everything without complaining or arguing, so that you may become blameless and pure, children of God without fault in a crooked and depraved generation, in which you shine like stars in the universe as you hold out the word of life” (Phil 2:14-16 NIV)

Paul also reminds us that we as 'children of light' should live self-controlled and godly lives in faith, hope and love. 

>“You are all sons of the light and sons of the day. We do not belong to the night or to the darkness” (1 Thess 5:5 NiV)

>“But since we belong to the day, let us be self-controlled, putting on faith and love as a breastplate, and the hope of salvation as a helmet” (1 Thess 5:8 NIV)


## 3.2 To the Glory of the Father (16b)

As we’ve already observed, the defining characteristic of an authentic light and works performed in genuine righteousness is that they won’t bring you praise, but rather the praise of our Heavenly Father (v16)

> .."and praise your Father in heaven" (Matt 5:16b NIV)

Our ultimate purpose as disciples of Christ is the glory of the Father. [^32] We desire to see men and women from all nations, bringing him praise, honour, and glory (δοξάζω). [^25] It's also important to notice the cosmic shift in language. While in the OT we read about the  “*God of Abraham*” or the “*LORD, God of our Fathers*” (Deut 26:7, 1 Chron 12:17, 1 Chron 29:10, 18; 2 Chron 13:12), to suddenly to  “Your Father in Heaven” (Matt 5:16). This is the first time in Scripture where God is referred to as our personal Father in heaven. Notice, therefore, the tremendous shift from worshipping the *God of Israel to praising the Father of Christ's disciples. Jesus' highest purpose in saving us, and in calling us to be the salt of the earth, and the light of the world, is that we, together with people from all nations, would bring the Father praise and glory (Matt 6:9).[^33]

> [!The Purpose of our Witness]
> 
“The goal of disciples’ witness is not that others emulate their way of life, or applaud their probity, but that they recognize the source of their distinctive lifestyle in "your Father in heaven”  (France 2007, 177 § Matt 5:16)

The Apostle Peter similarly calls believers to "*live such good lives among the pagans that, though they accuse you of doing wrong, they may see your good deeds and glorify God on the day he visits us”* (1 Pet 2:11-12 NIV)


# Summary

In summary, then,  discovered that disciples of Christ are the Light of the World（v14-16). Firstly, we are the Light of the World  (14a), because God is Light, Christ is Light, and now we shine the light of the Father and the Son through the Spirit. Secondly, we cannot hide our light (14b-15a), because collectively as the church of Christ, we are like a city on a hill that cannot be hidden. Similarly, as individual believers, it is absurd to have our lights lit by Christ, only to hide them; no, our purpose is to shine the light of the Gospel in our individual spheres of influence. Thirdly, so we must shine our light! (15-16), with both words and deeds. We must shine our Christian life and witness so that the Father is glorified, not our own names or ministries praised.


# Conclusion

In conclusion,  Jesus in vs 13-16 calls us, his disciples, to be the Salt of the Earth and the Light of the World. This is a calling to both participate with Christ in saving the world from sin and death, while at the same time we must take care not to lose our saltiness, nor to hide our light. We should maintain the tension of engaging deeply with the world while also maintaining our distinctiveness.[^30]  I therefore would like to encourage you to cultivate three disciplines that will help you maintain your distinctiveness as you pursue your goals. 

We must (1) dwell in the WORLD,  (2) dwell in the WORD, (3) dwell with ONE ANOTHER. [^26][^27]
## Dwell in the World
Continue to build relationships and engage with unbelievers and unbelieving communities.  This is where we can shine our light and radiate our fragrance as believers. [^28][^31] 
## Dwell in the Word
Take care, however, not to dwell so deeply in the World that you lose your distinctiveness. Instead, dwell and drink deeply from the Word of God so that you will maintain your distinctiveness. Take care that you stay connected with the Father and the Son through the Spirit.  This we do by drinking deeply and prayerfully from the Word of God. [^29]
## Dwell with One Another 
As individual disciples, we need help and encouragement from one another to shine our light and remain distinctive in the world.  

We therefore need regular and deep fellowship with one another,  so that we can shine both individually and collectively.  
May God give us grace to be his salt and light, and help us to keep our distinctiveness. Amen.
