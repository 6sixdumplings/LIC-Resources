## Introduction & Background 引言与背景

### Historical & Cultural Context 历史与文化背景
Friends, it is a joy to continue our journey through the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5–7) as we return to the Beatitudes for a third time. 

To understand the radical nature of Jesus' teaching, it is helpful to contrast His message with historical human paradigms of order and morality:

| Paradigm / 模式 | Historical Era / 历史时期 | Mechanism of Order / 秩序机制 | Core Limitation / 核心局限 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legalism / 法家** | Qin Dynasty (221–206 BCE) / 秦朝 | Imposed externally through strict laws (*Fa*), defined rewards, and harsh punishments | Produces a unified yet fear-driven state based on the assumption that human nature is selfish and unreliable |
| **Confucianism / 儒家** | Han Dynasty (206 BCE–220 CE, Emperor Wu) / 汉朝（汉武帝） | Social harmony cultivated through human virtue (*De*), benevolence (*Ren*), righteousness (*Yi*), and relational order | Relies on human moral effort (*Xiu Shen*) and self-cultivation, which cannot overcome the root of sin |
| **Christ's Kingdom / 基督天国** | Sermon on the Mount (Matt 5–7) / 登山宝训 | Internal transformation through a regenerated heart (*Regeneration & Grace*) | Requires divine grace; human virtue alone is insufficient—citizens of Christ's Kingdom require a transformed heart |

When Jesus unveils the manifesto of the Kingdom of Heaven, He makes it clear that true righteousness (*dikaiosynē*) cannot be achieved through external legalistic fear or mere human moral cultivation. True righteousness flows exclusively from a heart renewed by divine grace.

---

### Structural Review: Connected Pairs of the Beatitudes 八福的结构与逻辑联系

The Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3–10) represent the foundational manifesto of Jesus' Kingdom. As King of the Kingdom of Heaven, He outlines the fundamental heart attitudes, internal desires, and outward characteristics of His citizens.

Notice the logical connection between the first four Beatitudes (inner dispositions/root) and the second four Beatitudes (outward expressions/fruit):

* **Poor in Spirit (v.3) ──► Merciful (v.7):** Those who recognize their spiritual bankruptcy before God and receive His unmerited grace are transformed into instruments that extend tangible mercy to others.
* **Mourning Over Sin (v.4) ──► Pure in Heart (v.8):** Those who grieve over personal and systemic sin seek the cleansing work of Christ, producing single-minded devotion and moral purity.
* **The Meek (v.5) ──► Peacemakers (v.9):** Those who yield their self-will to God's authority become active ambassadors of reconciliation rather than self-assertive agitators.
* **Hungering for Righteousness (v.6) ──► Persecuted for Righteousness (v.10):** Those whose supreme passion is God's righteousness will inevitably clash with a world hostile to divine truth.

## 1. Blessed Are the Peacemakers  使人和睦的人有福了 (Matt 5:9)

> **Matthew 5:9 (NIV / CUVNP)**  
> 9 *"Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God."*  
> 9「使人和睦的人有福了！因为他们必称为神的儿子。」  
> *(Greek: μακάριοι οἱ εἰρηνοποιοί, ὅτι αὐτοὶ υἱοὶ θεοῦ κληθήσονται.)*

### 1.1 The Meaning of Peacemaking (*Eirēnopoios*)  使人和睦的含义
The Greek term *eirēnopoios* refers not to passive pacifism or the avoidance of conflict, but to the active creation of peace (*shalom*). Peacemakers are actively engaged in repairing the brokenness caused by human sin.

#### A. Reconciliation with God and Man 与神与人和好
As the Kingdom of Heaven breaks into the fallen world, it calls Christ's disciples to serve as ambassadors of reconciliation:
* **Vertical Reconciliation:** Calling men and women to be reconciled to God through faith in Christ (*2 Corinthians 5:20*: *"We are therefore Christ's ambassadors, as though God were making His appeal through us. We implore you on Christ's behalf: Be reconciled to God."*).
* **Horizontal Reconciliation:** Resolving human conflicts across societal, relational, and cultural spheres.

#### B. Three Key Conflict Areas Addressed in Matthew 5–7 登山宝训处理的三大冲突
1. **Persecution from Unbelievers (Matt 5:10–12):** Hostility encountered when living out kingdom values.
2. **Interpersonal Strife & Anger (Matt 5:21–26, 5:38–48, 7:1–6):** Settled resentment, insults (*Raca*), legal disputes, and judgmentalism.
3. **Marital Breakdown & Unfaithfulness (Matt 5:27–32):** The dissolution of covenantal relationships.

#### C. The OT Concept of *Shalom*  旧约“沙龙”（平安）的特质
Peacemaking aims at *shalom*—holistic restoration across relational, emotional, spiritual, social, and physical dimensions. Peacemaking fills the brokenness of human conflict with the gold of God's grace.

### 1.2 "For They Shall Be Called Sons of God"  因为他们必称为神的儿子 (v.9b)
In ancient Jewish thought, to be called a "son of" someone meant displaying the definitive family characteristics of the father. 

* **Family Trait of God:** God is the God of peace (*2 Thessalonians 3:16*), and Christ is the Prince of Peace (*Isaiah 9:6*). Therefore, actively pursuing peace is the definitive family trait of God's true children.

* **Identity and Attribution:** The verb "shall be called" (*klēthēsontai*) signifies divine attribution. Just as Abraham's faith was credited as righteousness and he was called "a friend of God" (*James 2:23*), peacemakers are recognized by God and the world as genuine heirs of the Kingdom.

## 2. Blessed Are the Persecuted for Righteousness' Sake / 为义受逼迫的人有福了 (Matt 5:10–12)

> **Matthew 5:10–12 (NIV / CUVNP)**  
> 10 *"Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness' sake, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven."*  
> 11 *"Blessed are you when people insult you, persecute you and falsely say all kinds of evil against you because of me."*  
> 12 *"Rejoice and be glad, because great is your reward in heaven, for in the same way they persecuted the prophets who were before you."*  
> 10「为义受逼迫的人有福了！因为天国是他们的。」  
> 11「人若因我辱骂你们，逼迫你们，捏造各样坏话毁谤你们，你们就有福了！」  
> 12「应当欢喜快乐，因为你们在天上的赏赐是大的。在你们以前的先知，人也是这样逼迫他们。」  
> *(Greek: μακάριοι οἱ δεδιωγμένοι ἕνεκεν δικαιοσύνης, ὅτι αὐτῶν ἐστιν ἡ βασιλεία τῶν οὐρανῶν.)*

### 2.1 The Nature of Kingdom Persecution (*Diōkō*)  逼迫的本质
The Greek verb *diōkō* in verse 10 describes systematic, intentional, and organized hostility against believers on account of their allegiance to Christ and their commitment to divine righteousness.

#### A. Global Reality & Statistics 全球迫害的真实境况
Persecution is a normal Christian experience for those who live out kingdom values (*2 Timothy 3:12*: *"Everyone who wants to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted"*). Open Doors statistical data underscores this ongoing reality:
* **Global Rate:** 1 in 7 Christians worldwide face high levels of persecution.
* **Africa:** 1 in 5 Christians face persecution.
* **Asia:** 2 in 5 Christians face persecution.

#### B. Pastoral Perspectives on Seasons of Rest  关于平顺时期的牧养提醒
* **John Calvin:** Advised that seasons of peace and repose should be used for deep spiritual reflection and mental preparation, so that when persecution arises, believers are thoroughly prepared to stand firm (*Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists*, Matt 5:10).
* **John MacArthur:** Warned that avoiding persecution entirely is often too easy—one simply has to live like the world, compromise righteousness, remain silent about the Gospel, and go along with sin (*MacArthur New Testament Commentary*, Matt 5:10–12).

### 2.2 Why the Persecuted Are Blessed  为何受逼迫的人是有福的
1. **Evidence of Citizenship (v.10b):** Suffering for righteousness provides clear evidence that believers belong to the Kingdom of Heaven (*"for theirs is the kingdom of heaven"*).
2. **Great Heavenly Reward (v.12a):** Earthly loss is far outweighed by eternal glory. Heaven's reward (*misthos*) far surpasses any temporary sacrifice made for Christ.
3. **Standing in Prophetic Alignment (v.12b):** Persecuted believers stand directly in the historical line of God's faithful witnesses and prophets who preceded them.
