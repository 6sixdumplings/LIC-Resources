# Background 背景

In Matt 5:17-20, Jesus made it clear that he did not come to abolish the Law. 在马太福音5:17-20里，耶稣清楚表明祂不是来废掉律法的。

In fact, he came to give the Law its full and correct meaning, while also fulfilling all its requirements. 事实上，祂是来赋予律法完整而正确的意义，并且成全它的一切要求。

Scripture presents Jesus as the New Moses, the true teacher and interpretor of God’s holy law. 圣经把耶稣描绘为新的摩西，是真正的教师和神圣律法的解释者。

In chapter five, Jesus establishes a repeating pattern: 在第五章里，耶稣设立了一个不断重复的模式：

>“You have heard that it was said … but I tell you …” 
>(see 5: 21, 27, 31, 33, 38, 43).
>“You have heard that it was said … but I tell you …”“你们听见有吩咐的话……但我告诉你们……” (see 见 5: 21, 27, 31, 33, 38, 43).


First, Jesus addresses two commands from the Ten Commandments—the prohibition against murder (5:21–26; cf. Exod 20:13) and against adultery (5:27–30; cf. Exod 20:14). 首先，耶稣谈到十诫中的两条命令——不可杀人（5:21–26；参见出埃及记20:13）和不可奸淫（5:27–30；参见出埃及记20:14）。

Next, Jesus turns to three societal issues rooted in the case laws of the Pentateuch, namely divorce (5:31–32; cf. Deut 24:1–4), oaths (5:33–37; cf. Lev 19:12; Num 30:2; Deut 23:21), and revenge/retaliation (5:38–42; cf. Exod 21:24; Lev 24:20; Deut 19:21). 接着，耶稣转向三项源自摩西五经案例律法的社会问题，即离婚（5:31–32；参见申命记24:1–4）、誓言（5:33–37；参见利未记19:12；民数记30:2；申命记23:21），以及报复（5:38–42；参见出埃及记21:24；利未记24:20；申命记19:21）。

Finally, Jesus culminates with the greatest command—love—expanding it beyond neighbour to include even enemies (5:43–48; cf. Lev 19:18). 最后，耶稣以最大的命令——爱——作为总结，并将爱扩展到不仅是邻舍，甚至包括仇敌（5:43–48；参见利未记19:18）。

The Jewish teachers of the law, ever since receiving the law on Sinai, often misinterpreted and misapplied God’s law. 自从在西奈山领受律法以来，犹太律法教师常常误解并错误应用神的律法。

They tended to interpret and apply God’s law legalistically and externally, without seeing the spirit of the law or the law’s heart intent. 他们往往以律法主义和外在的方式来解释和应用神的律法，而没有看见律法的精神或律法的核心意图。

Therefore, Jesus calls his disciples to a righteousness that far exceeds the righteousness of the Pharisees, 因此，耶稣呼召祂的门徒要有一种“超过法利赛人之义”的义，

He calls us to a righteousness that extends beyond merely external obedience of the law. 祂呼召我们追求一种超越单单外在遵守律法的义。

In the beatitudes (Matt 5:3-10), Jesus laid down the heart, the inner attitudes such as meekness, mercy, purity and peace-loving upon which the law of God rests. 在八福（马太福音5:3-10）中，耶稣奠定了律法所依靠的心——内在的态度，如温柔、怜悯、清心和爱好和平。

Matthew 5:21-48 is a profound portion of Scripture where Jesus corrects, reinterprets and intensifies the law in view of these inner attitudes of the Kingdom of God. 马太福音5:21-48是一段深刻的经文，耶稣在其中根据神国的这些内在态度来纠正、重新解释并强化律法。

Let’s now look at Matt 5:21-26, where Jesus reinterprets and intensifies the law against murder. 现在让我们来看马太福音5:21-26，耶稣在这里重新解释并强化了关于不可杀人的律法。

# 1. Resentful angry is murder 怨恨与愤怒就是谋杀 (5:21-22)

Notice, firstly, how Jesus corrects the view of the teachers of the law who limited murder to simply the physical act of killing someone (v21). 请注意，首先耶稣纠正了律法教师的观点，他们把杀人仅仅局限在实际的行为上，就是夺去一个人的生命（第21节）。


### 5:21a You have heard it said to the people long ago.. 
你们听见有吩咐古人的话，说

**Read 5:21a**
>“**21a** You have heard that it was said to the people long ago, **21b** ‘You shall not murder, **21c** and anyone who murders will be subject to judgment. 你们听见有吩咐古人的话，说：‘不可杀人’，又说：‘凡杀人的，难免受审判。

The phrase “to those of old” or the ancient ones (τοῖς ἀρχαίοις), refers to the Israelites who first received the Law at Sinai (Exod 20:13; Deut 5:17). “古人”或“古时候的人”（τοῖς ἀρχαίοις），指的是在西奈山首次领受律法的以色列人（出埃及记20:13；申命记5:17）。

These Israelites received God’s law at Mount Sinai in the desert, and it was affirmed to them again before they entered the Promised Land (France (2007, § Matt 5:21, 200). 这些以色列人在旷野的西奈山领受了神的律法，在进入应许之地之前又再次得到确认（France, 2007, 马太福音5:21, 200）。

Upon receiving the Law at Sinai, the Levites were appointed to teach it to the people, a duty later entrusted to the Scribes (Deut 33:10; 31:9–13). 在西奈山领受律法后，利未人被指定教导百姓，这项职责后来交托给文士（申命记33:10；31:9–13）。

Ezra the scribe for example devoted himself to the study, practice and teaching of God’s law. (Ezra 7:10) 例如，文士以斯拉专心研究、遵行并教导神的律法（以斯拉记7:10）。

We read in Nehemiah 8:7-8 how he “gave the sense” of the Law so that the people may understand it (Nehemiah 8:7-8). 我们在尼希米记8:7-8读到，他“讲明意思”，使百姓能够明白律法（尼希米记8:7-8）。

Despite centuries of teaching and interpretation, the Pharisees and the teachers of the Law continued to apply a legalistic and external interpretation of the law. 尽管经过几百年的教导和解释，法利赛人和律法教师仍然以律法主义和外在的方式来应用律法。

In this block of teaching, then Jesus sets out to correct how the OT laws were understood, applied, and taught, from their reception at Sinai to his current day. 因此，在这一段教导中，耶稣要纠正旧约律法从西奈山领受直到祂当时的时代被理解、应用和教导的方式。

To Jesus’ hearers, his teaching would have been shocking or even revolutionary. 对于耶稣的听众来说，祂的教导是令人震惊的，甚至是革命性的。

**Read 5:21b**
>“**21a** You have heard that it was said to the people long ago, **21b ‘You shall not murder,** **21c** and anyone who murders will be subject to judgment.’

### 5:21b 'You shall not murder'
‘不可杀人’

Beginning with the sixth command, Jesus deals with the command against murder. 从第六条诫命开始，耶稣处理关于不可杀人的命令。

We observe immediately that the words _“you shall not murder”_ is a direct quotation from Exodus 20:13 and Deuteronomy 5:17 in the LXX Septuagint (France 2007, § Matt 5:21, 200). 我们立刻注意到，“不可杀人”这句话直接引用自七十士译本中的出埃及记20:13和申命记5:17（France 2007，马太福音5:21, 200）。

### 5:21b "and anyone who murders will be subject to judgment" 
‘凡杀人的，难免受审判。’**

However, the second part of their teaching v21c is not a quote from OT scripture, but a summary of their application. 然而，他们在第21节后半部分的教导并不是引用旧约圣经，而是对他们应用的总结。

Read Matt 5:21c 

>**21a** You have heard that it was said to the people long ago, 21b ‘You shall not murder, 21c and anyone who murders will be subject to judgment 你们听见有吩咐古人的话，说：**‘不可杀人’，说：‘凡杀人的，难免受审判**。’


To “be subject to judgment” (Matt 5:21b) is a summary reference for the death penalty that was prescribed in the law (Gen 9:6; Exod 21:12–14; Lev 24:17; Num 35:30–31) (France 2007, § Matt 5:21). “难免受审判”（马太福音5:21b）是对律法中所规定死刑的概括性引用（创世记9:6；出埃及记21:12–14；利未记24:17；民数记35:30–31）（France 2007，马太福音5:21）。

Let’s read together: 让我们一同来读：

>**Genesis 9:6** Whoever sheds human blood, by humans shall their blood be shed; for in the image of God has God made mankind. 

>创世记 9:6** 凡流人血的，他的血也必被人所流，因为神造人是照自己的形像造的。

>**Exodus 21:12** Anyone who strikes a person with a fatal blow is to be put to death 

>**出埃及记 21:12** 打人以致打死的，必要把他治死。

>**Leviticus 24:17** Anyone who takes the life of a human being is to be put to death.

>**利未记 24:17** 凡打死人的，必被治死。


Human life is valuable exactly because every person in the world was created in the image of God. 人的生命之所以宝贵，正是因为世上的每一个人都是按着神的形象被造的。

Therefore, under the Mosaic Law, the principle of ‘life for life’ demanded a death penalty for murder. 因此，在摩西律法之下，“以命偿命”的原则要求对杀人者施行死刑。

As a result, the teachers of the law summarised their theology as “anyone who commits a physical murder will be subject to capital punishment by a human court.” 因此，律法教师把他们的神学总结为：“凡是实际杀人的人，必受人间法庭的死刑审判。”

While at first glance this might appear to be a correct interpretation of the Mosaic Law, there were two problems with their interpretative framework: 乍看之下，这似乎是对摩西律法的正确解释，但他们的诠释框架存在两个问题：

**Firstly**, the teachers of the law were faithful to the letter of the law, but they ignored its spirit. **第一**，律法教师忠于律法的字句，却忽略了律法的精神。

They took the intended ultimate boundary of behaviour, namely murder. They conveniently turned it into a very narrow definition of murder. 他们把律法所设定的行为终极界限——杀人，简化成一个非常狭窄的定义。

They ignored the vast array of thoughts, emotions, words, and actions that eventually lead to murder. 他们忽视了最终导致杀人的各种思想、情感、言语和行为。

**Secondly**, they reduce the judgment of murder to a human court’s judgment, while ignoring the wrath and judgment of God upon the lawbreakers. **第二**，他们把对杀人的审判缩减为人间法庭的审判，却忽略了神对违法者的忿怒和审判。

Jesus, as the new Moses and true Levite, clearly opposes this traditional interpretation of the law against murder. 耶稣作为新的摩西和真正的利未人，明确反对这种传统的杀人律法解释。

He expands the meaning of the law against murder to the inner recesses of the human heart, addressing the root issue of anger and resentment of the heart. 祂把不可杀人的律法意义扩展到人心深处，直面愤怒和怨恨的根源问题。

### Application  应用

You might have expected that these early interpreters of God’s law, as its original recipients, would be expected to comprehend the law accurately. 你或许会认为，这些最早领受神律法的人，作为律法的原始接受者，理应准确理解律法。

Yet they failed to apply God’s law correctly, because they applied it legalistically, externally, while ignoring the thoughts and emotions of the heart. 然而，他们并没有正确应用神的律法，因为他们只是在律法主义和外在层面上应用，而忽略了内心的思想和情感。

This invites us to approach Scripture, whether the OT or NT, with humility, because it’s so easy to get it wrong. 这提醒我们，无论是旧约还是新约，都必须以谦卑的心来读经，因为我们很容易误解。

We must guard against mere external or legalistic obedience while failing to see the sin of our hearts. 我们必须防范只停留在外在或律法主义的顺服，而忽略了内心的罪。

Also, as individual believers and collectively as the church of Christ, we must always return to Scripture. 同时，作为个人信徒和基督的教会整体，我们必须不断回到圣经。

We must keep returning to central matters of our faith to make sure our interpretation is in line with the teaching of Christ, and our application according to sound doctrine. 我们必须不断回到信仰的核心，确保我们的解释与基督的教导一致，并且我们的应用符合健全的教义。 

# 2. Resentful anger will be judged 怀恨的愤怒必受审判 (v22)。

### 5:22a **But I tell you** that anyone angry with his brother will be subject to judgment. 
但是我告诉你们：所有向他弟兄发怒的，当遭受审判

**Read 5:22a** 
>**But I tell you** that anyone angry with his brother will be subject to judgment. 但是我告诉你们：所有向他弟兄发怒的，当遭受审判

Notice the authority in Jesus’ words, _“but I tell you”_! (5:22a) 请注意耶稣话语中的权威： “但我告诉你们！”（5:22a）

As the new Moses, he is the true teacher, interpreter and fulfilment of God’s law. 祂作为新的摩西，是神律法真正的教师、解释者和成全者。

Green (2000, 94) notes that Jesus goes far deeper than the scribal tradition, addressing not merely the fruit of murder, but the root of anger. Green（2000, 94）指出，耶稣比文士的传统走得更深，不仅处理杀人的果子，更直指愤怒的根源。

> “Jesus goes far deeper. He traces murder to its dark lair in human hearts: hatred. The God who sees in secret is affronted not only by the fruit, murder, but by the root, hatred” (Green 2000, 94). “耶稣走得更深。他把杀人追溯到人心黑暗的巢穴：仇恨。那位在暗中察看的神，不仅因杀人的果子而愤怒，也因仇恨的根而愤怒。”（Green 2000, 94）

It is not merely the act of killing a brother or sister that brings us under judgment, but even so small a matter as having anger and resentment against a brother or sister. 使我们落在审判之下的不仅仅是杀害弟兄姊妹的行为，甚至是心中对弟兄姊妹的愤怒和怨恨。

Jesus refers to the festering anger that flows from hurt and unforgiveness. 耶稣指出那种因伤害和不饶恕而滋生的愤怒。

When we refuse to forgive, our hurt and anger escalate to resentment. 当我们拒绝饶恕时，伤痛和愤怒就会升级为怨恨。

Our resentment turns negative thoughts into insulting words and public slander. 怨恨会把负面的思想转化为辱骂的言语和公开的毁谤。

Our threatening words may well turn into a violent action. 我们的威胁性言语甚至可能演变为暴力行为。

Some translations of Matt 5:22 include the phrase _“angry without a cause.”_ 一些马太福音5:22的译本包含“无缘无故地发怒”这一短语。

Stott (1985, 83 § Matt 5:22) points out that anger “without a cause” appears in many manuscripts but not in the earliest ones. He concludes it’s a later addition, although he believes the scribes rightly understood Jesus’ intention. Stott（1985, 83，马太福音5:22）指出，“无缘无故地发怒”出现在许多抄本中，但不在最早的抄本里。他认为这是后来的增补，尽管他相信文士们正确理解了耶稣的意图。

### 5:22b Again, anyone who says to his brother, ‘Raca,’ is answerable to the Sanhedrin 
骂他弟兄‘废物’的，当遭受议会的审判. 

**Read 5:22b** 
>Again, anyone who says to **his brother,** ‘Raca,’ is answerable to the Sanhedrin. 骂他弟兄‘废物’的，当遭受议会的审判

We know that Jesus’ audience included both his twelve disciples and the crowds following him. 我们知道，耶稣的听众包括祂的十二个门徒和跟随祂的群众。

So when Jesus refers to “_anyone who says to his brother_” he may primarily have in mind followers and disciples of Christ. 因此，当耶稣提到“凡对弟兄说话的人”时，祂主要想到的是基督的跟随者和门徒。

Jesus is therefore setting the standard of Christian living for his disciples as citizens of the Kingdom of Heaven (France 2007, §Matt 5:22, 200). 因此，耶稣为祂的门徒设立了作为天国子民的基督徒生活标准（France 2007，马太福音5:22, 200）。

We will see this theme of forgiveness and reconciliation re-emerge throughout Matthew’s Gospel (France 2007, §Matt 5:22, 200). 我们会在整本马太福音中再次看到饶恕与和好的主题不断出现（France 2007，马太福音5:22, 200）。

**Read 5:22b**
>Again, anyone who says to his brother, **‘Raca,’** is answerable to the Sanhedrin. 骂他弟兄‘**废物**’的，当遭受议会的审判

Raca means calling someone worthless, good‑for‑nothing, or a fool (Fensham 1996, 998). “拉加”意思是称某人为无价值、无用或愚蠢的人（Fensham 1996, 998）。

An angry, resentful heart often expresses itself in angry and abusive words. 心怀怨怒的心常常通过愤怒和辱骂的言语表现出来。

Such angry resentment expressed in insults and slander is murder! 这种通过辱骂和毁谤所表现出来的愤怒与怨恨，就是杀人！

**Read 5:22b**
Again, anyone who says to his brother, ‘Raca,’ **is answerable to the Sanhedrin 骂他弟兄‘废物’的，当遭受议会的审判

We must keep in mind that Jesus is speaking to his Jewish audience for whom the Sanhedrin was the highest legal court for the Jews in Jerusalem (Thompson 1996, 1060). 我们必须记住，耶稣是在对犹太听众讲话，而在耶路撒冷，公会（Sanhedrin）是犹太人最高的法律法庭（Thompson 1996, 1060）。 //

In other words, Jesus is making the point that angry insults ought to be treated with the same seriousness in the supreme court as physical murder. 换句话说，耶稣强调，愤怒的辱骂应当在最高法庭中被视为与实际杀人同样严重的罪行。 //

### 5: 22c But anyone who says, ‘You fool!’ will be in danger of the fire of hell. 
骂‘蠢货’的，当遭受烈火的地狱。

**Read 5:22c** 
>But anyone who says, ‘**You fool!’** will be in danger of the fire of hell 骂‘蠢货’的，当遭受烈火的地狱。

Jesus underlines that if you insult a neighbour or a fellow disciple of Christ, it is a serious matter. 耶稣强调，如果你辱骂邻舍或基督的门徒，这是极其严重的事情。

When you demean someone in your heart, or express with your words that someone is an idiot, fool, moron or worthless—it violates the law of God against murder. 当你在心里轻视别人，或用言语称某人为愚蠢、无用或毫无价值时——你就违反了神关于不可杀人的律法。

When you employ such angry words against your spouse or children, your co‑workers or supervisor, or someone who cut in before you while driving—you make yourself liable to be disciplined or judged by God. 当你用这样的愤怒言语对待配偶或孩子、同事或上司，或是在开车时插队的人——你就使自己落在神的管教和审判之下。

Always keep in mind that God created all people in his image. 要常常记住，神按着祂的形象创造了所有人。

The Father loved and foreknew your fellow believers from before the creation of the world. 在创世以前，天父就爱并预知了你的弟兄姊妹。

Christ redeemed them by his precious blood. 基督用祂宝贵的血救赎了他们。

The Spirit of God lives in them, and they are sealed for an eternal inheritance with you. 神的灵住在他们里面，他们也与您一同被印记，得着永恒的产业。

So who are you to judge, who are you to speak so abusively against your brother who will inherit eternity with you? 那么，你是谁，竟敢论断、辱骂那位将要与你一同承受永恒的弟兄呢？

Yes, he or she may have sinned and caused your hurt, but the chances are that you have done the same. 是的，他或她可能犯罪并伤害了你，但很可能你也曾做过同样的事。

Therefore, as Christ’s disciples, we must avoid at all cost becoming hardened in anger and resentment. 因此，作为基督的门徒，我们必须竭力避免让愤怒和怨恨在心中变得刚硬。

We must repent of our angry and harmful words—especially at home, at church, and towards our fellow believers. 我们必须为愤怒和伤人的言语悔改——特别是在家里、在教会里，以及对待我们的弟兄姊妹时。

The fire of hell is a translation of the Greek _geënna_, which refers to the Valley of Hinnom. “地狱的火”是希腊文 _geënna_ 的翻译，指的是欣嫩谷。

The Hinnom Valley served as a burning rubbish dump outside and below Jerusalem. 欣嫩谷曾是耶路撒冷城外和城下的垃圾焚烧场。

Its constantly burning rubbish provided a vivid image of the eternal fire of hell prepared for the devil, his angels and unbelievers (Matt 25:41) (France 2007, 202 § Matt 5:22). 那不断燃烧的垃圾，生动地描绘了为魔鬼、他的使者和不信之人所预备的永火（马太福音25:41）（France 2007，马太福音5:22）。

Jesus is not saying that once‑off or occasional anger brings us into eternal judgment. 耶稣并不是说一次性的或偶尔的愤怒就会使我们落入永恒的审判。

Instead he presents us with a very serious warning: 祂给我们一个极其严肃的警告：

If you allow anger, resentment and unforgiveness to take root in your heart, it will consume your heart, mind and emotions, and may suffocate your faith. 如果你让愤怒、怨恨和不饶恕在心里扎根，它会吞噬你的心思意念和情感，甚至可能窒息你的信心。

You face the risk of being no different than unbelievers, and consequently at risk of the fire of hell. 你就面临与不信之人无异的危险，最终落在地狱的火焰之中。

Whether or not we will face the judgment of hell depends upon our faith in Christ and reconciliation with the Father. 我们是否会面对地狱的审判，取决于我们是否信靠基督并与天父和好。

Yet if our lives show no evidence of forgiveness and reconciliation with fellow believers, it seriously questions the genuineness of our faith—which means we are at risk of the fire of hell (Green 2000, 94). 然而，如果我们的生活没有表现出对弟兄姊妹的饶恕与和好，就会严重质疑我们信心的真实性——这意味着我们正处在地狱火焰的危险之中（Green 2000, 94）。

Last week we saw the destructive impact of resentment and bitterness in Absalom’s life. 上周我们看到了怨恨和苦毒在押沙龙生命中的毁灭性影响。

Likewise, hardening your heart against a brother in unforgiveness, resentment and hatred makes you a murderer in God’s eyes. 同样地，在不饶恕、怨恨和仇恨中刚硬你的心，在神眼中你就是一个杀人者。

Stott (1985, 85 § Matt 5:22) pointedly draws out the connection between anger and murder. Stott（1985, 85，马太福音5:22）明确指出愤怒与杀人的联系。

“Anger and insult are ugly symptoms of a desire to get rid of somebody who stands in our way. Our thoughts, looks and words all indicate that, as we sometimes dare to say, we ‘wish he were dead’. Such an evil wish is a breach of the sixth commandment” (Stott 1985, 85 § Matt 5:22). “愤怒和辱骂是想要除掉挡在我们面前之人的丑陋症状。我们的思想、眼神和言语都表明，有时我们甚至敢说，‘但愿他死了’。这种邪恶的愿望就是违反第六条诫命。”（Stott 1985, 85，马太福音5:22）。

Such hardened unforgiveness and anger demonstrate that you live in spiritual darkness, and are not in the light (1 John 3:15). 这种刚硬的不饶恕和愤怒表明你活在属灵的黑暗中，而不在光明里（约翰一书3:15）。

## What about righteous anger?  
那么，正义的愤怒又如何呢？

We must also understand that not all anger is sinful, nor should we avoid conflict or problems. 我们必须明白，并非所有的愤怒都是罪，也不应当逃避冲突或问题。

God has given each of us the ability to experience hurt and anger, which are normal human emotions. 神赐给我们每个人感受伤害和愤怒的能力，这些都是正常的人类情感。

Godly anger can help us discern when something is wrong, or when an injustice has taken place. 敬虔的愤怒能帮助我们分辨错误，或察觉到不公义的事情发生。

We use the energy of godly anger to resolve a problem, handle conflict and correct a wrong. 我们可以用敬虔愤怒的力量来解决问题、处理冲突并纠正错误。

However, righteous anger draws its energy from God’s holiness, a pursuit of righteousness and justice, and is filled with God’s love and mercy. 然而，公义的愤怒源自神的圣洁，追求公义和正义，并充满神的爱与怜悯。

Luther described righteous anger as an anger that is “friendly to the person but hostile to the sin” (Stott 1985, 84 § Matt 5:22). 路德形容公义的愤怒是“对人友善，但对罪敌对的愤怒”（Stott 1985, 84，马太福音5:22）。

To the contrary, Jesus in Matt 5:22 deals with a settled anger, a malice that is nursed inwardly, an anger of pride, vanity, hatred and revenge (Stott 1985, 84 § Matt 5:22). 相反，耶稣在马太福音5:22所处理的是一种根深蒂固的愤怒，一种在心中滋养的恶意，是骄傲、虚荣、仇恨和报复的愤怒（Stott 1985, 84，马太福音5:22）。

This type of anger, resentment, and verbal abuse must be dealt with quickly and decisively, as we will see in verses 23–24 (Wiersbe 1996, 23). 这种愤怒、怨恨和言语上的伤害必须迅速而果断地处理，正如我们将在23–24节中看到的（Wiersbe 1996, 23）。

### 5:23a If you are offering your gift at the altar (v23-24)

**Read 5:23a** 
>23a “Therefore, **if you are offering your gift at the altar** 23b and there remember that your brother has something against you, 23a 所以，你在祭坛上献礼物的时候，23b 若想起弟兄向你怀怨，

_“Offering your gift at the altar (v23a)”_ refers to any burnt offering, sin offering or free‑will offering (Lev 1–5) that the Jews would bring to the temple in Jerusalem to seek forgiveness of their sins or in thanks or worship of God. “在祭坛上献礼物”（23a）指的是任何燔祭、赎罪祭或甘心祭（利未记1–5），这是犹太人带到耶路撒冷圣殿，为了寻求罪得赦免，或向神表达感谢与敬拜。

The OT prophets, like Isaiah and Amos, denounced the people for their hypocrisy in observing their religious practices while being unjust to each other (Isaiah 1:11–17, Amos 5:21–24). 旧约先知如以赛亚和阿摩司，曾谴责百姓在遵守宗教仪式时却彼此不公的虚伪（以赛亚书1:11–17，阿摩司书5:21–24）。

For example in Isaiah 1:13 we read: 例如，在以赛亚书1:13我们读到：

>“Stop bringing meaningless offerings! Your incense is detestable to me. New Moons, Sabbaths and convocations—I cannot bear your worthless assemblies.” “你们不要再献虚浮的供物！香品是我所憎恶的；月朔、安息日并宣召的大会，我不能容忍虚假的聚会。”


God hated their sacrifices, assemblies and feasts, because although outwardly they upheld their religion, inwardly they were spiritually dead and relationships with one another broken. 神厌恶他们的祭物、聚会和节期，因为他们外表上维持着宗教的形式，内心却是属灵的死亡，人与人之间的关系也已经破裂。

Fractured relationships with one another are a sign of a broken relationship with God. 人与人之间关系的破裂，正是与神关系破裂的明显标志。

### 5:23b and there remember that your brother has something against you

**Read Matt 5:23b**
>23a “Therefore, if you are offering your gift at the altar 23b **and there remember that your brother has something against you**, 23a 所以，你在祭坛上献礼物的时候，23b 若想起弟兄向你怀怨，

In verses 21–22 Jesus addressed the anger we feel toward another person. 在21–22节中，耶稣处理的是我们对他人的愤怒。

Yet here in verses 23–24 the direction shifts—the anger of a brother or sister against us. 然而在23–24节中，方向发生了转变——是弟兄或姊妹对我们的愤怒。

The illustration paints a vivid picture of an Israelite entering the temple to present his offering before God. 这个比喻生动地描绘了一个以色列人进入圣殿，在神面前献祭的情景。

In that solemn moment, just before the animal is slaughtered and burned on the altar, he remembers that a fellow Israelite or brother holds something against him. 在那庄严的时刻，就在祭牲被宰杀并焚烧在祭坛之前，他想起有一位同胞或弟兄对他怀有怨恨。

Jesus does not say whether the worshipper is innocent or guilty. 耶稣并没有说明这位敬拜者是无辜还是有罪。

He only says that he remembers a broken relationship or an offence that someone holds against him, which now hinders his worship of God. 祂只是说，这人记起了一段破裂的关系，或有人对他心怀不满，而这阻碍了他对神的敬拜。

It is possible that there had already been a cycle of hurt, anger, and slander between them, and the resentment is mutual. 很可能他们之间已经经历过伤害、愤怒和毁谤的循环，怨恨是彼此的。

Perhaps he did something or said something unintentionally, but it still caused hurt and resentment in his brother. 也许他无意中做了或说了什么，但仍然让弟兄受伤并心生怨恨。

Either way, there is a broken relationship, marked by anger and hurt. 无论如何，这是一段因愤怒和伤害而破裂的关系。

So the question arises: should he continue to present his burnt offering before God? 那么问题来了：他是否还应继续在神面前献上燔祭呢？

Stop faking a reconciled relationship with God, while your life is marked by broken relationships with your brothers and sisters. 不要假装与你神的关系已经和好，而你的生命却充满了与弟兄姊妹破裂的关系。

The Galilean Jews who were listening to Jesus would have been struck by the radical implication of His teaching. 加利利的犹太人听到耶稣的教导时，一定会被其中的激烈含义所震撼。

For them, going to the temple and bringing a gift to the altar meant an 80‑mile journey to Jerusalem, usually requiring a week’s travel on foot. 对他们来说，去圣殿献祭意味着要步行约八十英里到耶路撒冷，通常需要一周的旅程。

# 4  Settle matters with your adversary 与你的对手和解 (v25) 

In verses 25–26 we see the same principle reinforced, though in a different context. 在25–26节中，我们看到同样的原则被再次强调，只是处境不同。

The first example (vv. 23–24) concerns a brother in the context of worship, while the second example involves an adversary in the marketplace. 第一个例子（23–24节）涉及在敬拜场景中的弟兄，而第二个例子则涉及在市集中的对头。

Yet in both situations someone holds something against you. 然而，在这两种情况下，都有人对你怀有怨恨。

The situation calls for immediate and decisive action to avoid hardening the conflict and facing more serious consequences (Stott 1985, 85 § Matt 5:23). 这种情况需要立刻采取果断的行动，以避免矛盾恶化并导致更严重的后果（Stott 1985, 85，马太福音5:23）。

## 5:25a Settle matters with your adversary or accuser 
与你的对手或控告者和解

**Read 5:25a** 
>25a “Settle matters quickly with your **adversary** who is taking you to court. 你同告你的对头还在路上，就赶紧与他和息，

Here in verses 25–26 the disciple of Christ seems to be facing a more serious and hostile conflict. 在第25至26节中，基督的门徒似乎正面临一场更为严峻且充满敌意的冲突。

There is an accuser or adversary (ἀντίδικος antidikos) who desires to drag him off to court. 这里有一个“控告者”或“对头”（ἀντίδικος antidikos），想要把他拉去法庭。

This is therefore not a conflict the disciple seeks or desires to enter into—it is forced upon him. 因此，这并不是门徒主动寻求或愿意进入的冲突，而是被迫卷入其中。

We can easily imagine believers today facing such serious conflicts that threaten legal action. 我们很容易想象今日的信徒面对类似的严重冲突，甚至威胁到法律诉讼：

 A Christian husband or wife, after a season of conflict, hurt, and resentment, decides to sue their spouse for divorce. 一位基督徒丈夫或妻子，在经历一段冲突、伤害和怨恨之后，决定起诉配偶离婚。
 
A Christian family faces financial misfortune and defaults on their home loan payment. The bank threatens to repossess their home.  一个基督徒家庭遭遇经济困境，无法偿还房贷，银行威胁要收回他们的房屋。

A business owner, for any reason, fails to deliver on an agreement or breaches a contract, resulting in a lawsuit from their client. 一位基督徒企业主因某种原因未能履行协议或违约，导致客户提起诉讼。

## 5:25b Settle quickly while you still have the opportunity
趁现在还有机会，赶紧达成和解吧

**Read Matt 5:25b** 
>25b “Do it while you are still together on the way, or your adversary may hand you over to the judge, and the judge may hand you over to the officer, and you may be thrown into prison. 头还在路上，就赶紧与他和息，恐怕他把你送给审判官，审判官交付衙役，你就下在监里了。

Again notice the legal terminology of being _handed over_ (**παραδίδωμι paradidomi**) and being judged in a court of law (**κριτής krites**). 再次注意这里的法律用语：**“被交出”**（παραδίδωμι _paradidomi_）以及在法庭上**“被审判”**（κριτής _krites_）。

Again, notice the legal terminology of being _handed over_ (**παραδίδωμι paradidomi**) and being judged in a court of law (**κριτής krites**). 再次注意这里的法律用语：被“交出”（**παραδίδωμι paradidomi**）以及在法庭上被“审判”（**κριτής krites**）。

I’m not certain if Jesus’ purpose in this brief parable was to provide us with legal advice. 我不确定耶稣在这个简短的比喻中是否意在给我们法律上的建议。

Instead, He might simply be illustrating the importance of taking decisive action to reconcile with an accuser or adversary. 祂可能只是要说明：与控告者或对头和好，必须采取果断的行动。

It calls us to take action to settle matters while there is still time, before the consequences become severe. 这呼召我们要趁着还有机会，赶紧解决问题，免得后果变得严重。

The parable set in a legal context urges that “a settlement out of court” is “a more prudent option” than “the prospect of imprisonment” (France 2007, 203 § Matt 5:25–27). 这个设在法律背景中的比喻强调，“庭外和解”比“坐牢的前景”更为明智（France 2007, 203，马太福音5:25–27）。

Jesus’ purpose here is not to give us legal advice, but to reinforce an ethical message: _do not allow bad relationships to remain unresolved._ 耶稣在此的目的并不是提供法律建议，而是要强调一个伦理信息：**不要让破裂的关系一直未被解决。**

Perhaps the point Jesus is making here is that in a broken relationship—whether with a Christian brother or an unbelieving adversary—there is usually a window of opportunity to resolve the issue and restore the situation. 也许耶稣在这里要表达的重点是：在破裂的关系中——无论是与基督徒弟兄，还是与不信的对头——通常都会有一个机会窗口来解决问题并恢复关系。

However, if through neglect or hardened anger you fail to seize the opportunity, the conflict may become entrenched, leading to severe consequences. 然而，如果因疏忽或顽固的愤怒而错过机会，冲突可能会根深蒂固，最终导致严重的后果。

We often see this even in Christian marriages that end in divorce. 我们常常在基督徒婚姻中看到这种情况，最终以离婚收场。

One or both parties avoid resolving conflicts, allowing hurt, resentment, and unforgiveness to build until the relationship becomes toxic, leading to divorce. 夫妻双方之一或两人都逃避解决冲突，让伤害、怨恨和不饶恕不断累积，直到关系变得有毒，最终导致离婚。

## 5:26 Settle before facing the full consequences
在面临全部后果之前先达成和解

**Read 5:26**
>26 I tell you the truth, you will not get out until you have paid the last penny. 你同告你的对头还在路上，就赶紧与他和息，恐怕他把你送给审判官，审判官交付衙役，你就下在监里了。

Failure to take early and decisive action toward reconciliation, and missing the window of opportunity, will result in serious consequences. 若未能及早采取果断的行动去和好，错过机会之窗，就会带来严重的后果。

Failing to make use of your window of opportunity for reconciliation will result in having to pay the full debt (ἀποδίδωμι _apodidomi_), or bearing the full weight of the consequences. 若不把握和好的机会，就必须偿还全部的债务（ἀποδίδωμι _apodidomi_），或承担全部的后果。

It certainly seems from this short parable that Jesus does not desire His disciples to be in and out of the court—certainly not with fellow believers, nor with unbelievers. 从这个简短的比喻来看，耶稣显然不希望祂的门徒频繁出入法庭——无论是与信徒，还是与不信的人。

Followers of Christ want to be the salt of the earth and the light of the world, and so we want to avoid as far as possible such entanglements that will harm our witness. 基督的跟随者要作世上的盐、世界的光，因此我们要尽可能避免那些会损害见证的纠纷。

So Jesus urges us, as His disciples, that where we have offended an adversary—likely an unbeliever—we should take extreme care to settle matters before they go to court (Poole 1853, 24 § Matt 5:25–26). 因此，耶稣劝勉我们作为祂的门徒，若我们冒犯了一个对头——很可能是不信的人——就必须极其谨慎，在事情进入法庭之前解决问题（普尔 1853, 24，马太福音5:25–26）。

Yet there may also be a deeper application here. 然而，这里也可能有更深的应用。

## 5:26 Reconcile with God before being judged in hell 
在地狱受审判之前，先与神和解

**Read 5:26a**
>26 **I tell you the truth,** you will not get out until you have paid the last penny. 我实在告诉你，若有一文钱没有还清，你断不能从那里出来。」

In using the phrase _“I tell you the truth”_ (v.26), Jesus may be indicating that there is a higher purpose behind this warning. 在使用“我实在告诉你们”（第26节）这一短语时，耶稣可能表明这警告背后有更高的目的。

He may be reminding the crowds—those who had not yet become His followers—that ultimately God Himself is your adversary. 祂可能是在提醒群众——那些尚未成为祂门徒的人——最终神自己就是你们的对头。

Your sin, anger, and broken relationships bring you under the judgment of God. 你的罪、愤怒和破裂的关系使你落在神的审判之下。

It is therefore prudent, and it calls you to decisive action, to settle matters with God and be reconciled to Him through faith in Christ—so that your sins may be forgiven. 因此，明智的做法是采取果断的行动，与神解决问题，并借着信靠基督与祂和好——好使你的罪得赦免。

If you ignore this window of opportunity to settle matters with God, to be reconciled with Him through the Lord Jesus Christ, 如果你忽视了这个与神解决问题、借着主耶稣基督与祂和好的机会之窗，

you will have to bear the full consequences of your sin, and pay the full price of your sin in eternal hell. 你就必须承担罪的全部后果，并在永远的地狱里付出罪的全部代价。

Jesus therefore urges us to make sure that we have settled matters with God, our adversary. 因此，耶稣劝勉我们要确保已经与神——我们的对头——解决了问题。

It urges us to reconcile with the Father, by bringing our debt of sin to the Lord Jesus Christ in faith. [^40] 这劝勉我们要借着信心，把我们的罪债带到主耶稣基督面前，与天父和好。 [^40]
